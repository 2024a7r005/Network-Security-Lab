# Experiment 8: Send and Receive PGP Encrypted Email using Thunderbird

## Aim
To securely send and receive encrypted and digitally signed emails using Thunderbird and OpenPGP.

## Overview
This experiment demonstrates secure email communication using OpenPGP in Mozilla Thunderbird. Public and private key pairs are generated and exchanged to enable encryption and digital signature verification.

## Tools Required
- Mozilla Thunderbird
- OpenPGP (Built-in support)
- Two test email accounts

## Procedure
1. Installed and configured Mozilla Thunderbird.
2. Enabled the Primary Password to protect local key storage.
3. Generated an OpenPGP public and private key pair.
4. Recorded the key fingerprint and expiry date.
5. Exported and exchanged public keys with a lab partner.
6. Imported the partner's public key and verified its fingerprint.
7. Sent an email with encryption and digital signature enabled.
8. Received and decrypted the email using the private key.
9. Verified the digital signature and sent an encrypted reply.

## Key Concepts
- **Encryption:** Protects email confidentiality.
- **Digital Signature:** Verifies message integrity and the signing key.
- **Public Key:** Used for encryption and signature verification.
- **Private Key:** Used for decryption and signing.
- **Fingerprint:** Helps verify the authenticity of a public key.

## Result
Successfully exchanged OpenPGP public keys and sent and received encrypted, digitally signed emails using Thunderbird. Message decryption and digital signature verification were also performed.

## Conclusion
The experiment demonstrated how OpenPGP provides confidentiality, integrity and key-based authentication in email communication.
